@michaelxpettis · 请求账号 @michaelxpettis · 2026-08-05 08:55:02 UTC · 引用原帖：I would make Tordoir's point more broadly. German experiences provide an especially good set of examples on whether it is "innovation" or mercantilist labor policies are more likely to drive manufacturing competitiveness. Germany switched from small deficits and lower manufacturing competitiveness before 2003-05 to a rapid surge in trade surpluses and competitiveness thereafter. During the late 2010s, however, both once again started to decline. Given that the 2003-05 labor reforms resulted in a sharp decline in German wages relative to productivity, was the subsequent surge in Germany's manufacturing competitiveness driven by the growing gap between wages and productivity, or was it driven by a sudden increase in German innovation skills and a greater innate German ability to invent more advanced technology? If the latter, why did it last only a decade or so? By the middle of the 2010s, tight German labor markets resulted in a gradual recovery of wages relative to earlier productivity growth. Was the subsequent gradual loss of Germany's manufacturing competitiveness the result of the closing of the gap between wages and productivity, or did Germans around this time began to lose their ability to innovate and to invent more advanced technology? The point is that innovation and advancements in technology are important because they cause overall productivity to rise, and they allow certain manufacturing sectors, where the innovation is concentrated, to become relatively more competitive than other manufacturing sectors in that country. But they do not necessarily lead to all manufacturing sectors in a country becoming more competitive than the manufacturing sectors of that country's trade partners. That only happens when wages in that country are lower relative to productivity than they are in the rest of the world. Technological innovation is of course a great thing, but while it can explain sectoral trade surpluses, it has never explained systemic trade surpluses.↗
